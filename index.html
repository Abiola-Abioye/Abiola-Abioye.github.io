REC-01

Solar Energy Potential Analysis Across Alberta, ML, PV Simulation & Power BI

Python Random Forest pvlib Power BI

The Business Problem: Alberta has strong but geographically and seasonally variable solar resources, but raw hourly weather-station data isn't directly usable by non-technical decision-makers. Framed around a client (the Alberta Utilities Commission), the project needed to turn station-level irradiance data into city comparisons, predictive evidence, installation-level yield estimates, financial payback ranges, and long-term trend information for regulators, municipalities, and solar developers.

The Solution: Completed with teammates Samuel Aduaka and Prutha Chokshi. Built a full Python pipeline across seven notebooks: cleaned and validated 701,280 hourly weather records (1998-2017) across four Alberta cities alongside over a million raw SAIT campus solar readings, engineered cyclical time features (hour and day-of-year sine/cosine encoding) plus geographic and meteorological predictors, then trained and tuned a Random Forest model on a chronological train/validation/test split to predict hourly solar irradiance without temporal leakage. Simulated panel-tilt optimization and annual energy yield using the physics-based pvlib library, modeled simple payback under best/base/worst financial scenarios, and tested for statistically significant long-term irradiance trends with Mann-Kendall testing. Surfaced the results through a four-page Power BI dashboard for city comparisons, seasonal patterns, and payback economics.

Strategic Impact: The tuned Random Forest model exceeded the project's target of R² ≥ 0.85, reaching R² = 0.9169 on held-out test data (MAE 36.6 Wh/m², RMSE 69.5 Wh/m²), with all four cities individually clearing R² > 0.90. Optimized fixed panel tilt ranged from 39° to 42° across cities, with Lethbridge showing both the highest annual solar resource (1,687.64 kWh/m²/year) and the shortest base-case payback (13.23 years). Edmonton was the only city with a statistically significant increasing irradiance trend across the two-decade record (p = 0.023). Since the historical weather data ended in 2017 while usable SAIT campus solar readings only began in 2019, the team built a climatological consistency check instead of a false direct validation, comparing normalized seasonal/diurnal profiles between the two datasets and finding a strong Pearson correlation of r = 0.9686.

REC-03

Employee Timesheet Data Warehouse, SCD Type 1 & 2

SQL Server Data Warehousing Star Schema ETL

The Business Problem: Employee records change over time, contact details update, employees change departments or get promoted, but historical timesheet data still needs to reflect the correct department and role for the day the work was actually done, not just an employee's current profile.

The Solution: Built a staging-to-warehouse ETL pipeline in SQL Server around a star schema (DimEmployee, FactTimesheet). Implemented Slowly Changing Dimensions logic: Type 1 overwrites for low-impact fields like email and phone, and Type 2 versioning for department and job title changes, expiring the old record and inserting a new one with its own effective date range. Timesheet facts are then mapped to whichever employee version was active on the date the work occurred.

Strategic Impact: The model preserves accurate point-in-time reporting instead of overwriting history. For example, the warehouse can correctly show that one employee's hours split 28.57% under her old department and 71.43% under her new one after a transfer, a distinction a simpler "current state only" table would erase entirely.

REC-04

Financial Planning & Forecasting Model, NaturaGlow

Excel Goal Seek Scenario Analysis Forecasting

The Business Problem: NaturaGlow needed to know whether a new product line would be profitable before committing to it, but the answer depended heavily on volatile assumptions like unit price and expected sales volume, and a colleague's early sales estimate hadn't yet been checked against actual historical trends.

The Solution: Built a dynamic financial model in Excel with base-case profit and break-even calculations, then used Goal Seek to solve backward for the unit price and sales volume needed to hit specific profit targets. Layered in a two-variable sensitivity data table to stress-test profit against simultaneous swings in price and volume, and used Scenario Manager to compare Pessimistic, Most Likely, and Optimistic outcomes across two pricing tiers. Cross-checked all of it against a 5-year historical sales trendline forecast.

Strategic Impact: The base case revealed a projected $5,000 loss, with break-even actually requiring 1,250 units sold monthly. Goal Seek showed a $70 price point (up from a $50 base) would hit a $15,000 profit target, while at the base price, 2,250 units would be needed for a $20,000 profit. Scenario analysis found only the Most Likely High ($27,120) and Optimistic High ($31,880) scenarios cleared a $25,000 profit bar. The trendline forecast also showed a colleague's initial 1,000-unit sales estimate was overly conservative against the model's 1,303-unit "Most Likely" projection, meaning the business was positioned to outperform their planning baseline by over 30%.

REC-05

Predictive Analytics for Customer Retention, Telco Churn (MLR & kNN in R)

R Multiple Linear Regression kNN CRISP-DM

The Business Problem: In the telecom sector, acquiring a new subscriber costs significantly more than retaining an existing one. The business needed a way to forecast expected customer revenue and flag at-risk accounts before they cancel, using a dataset of 7,043 customer billing and service records.

The Solution: Completed with teammates Prutha Chokshi and Samuel Aduaka as part of SAIT's Data Analytics program. Followed the CRISP-DM framework end to end: ran a 5W1H stakeholder analysis to align the Finance and Retention teams around "revenue leakage," then cleaned and encoded the dataset in R, removing 11 incomplete billing records, binary-encoding churn status, and mapping contract length to an ordinal scale. Built two models to answer two business questions: a Multiple Linear Regression predicting a customer's total lifetime billing from tenure and monthly charges, and a kNN-based visual clustering approach segmenting customers by churn risk across those same two variables.

Strategic Impact: The regression model confirmed tenure and monthly charges as highly significant predictors of total revenue (p < 0.001), supporting an early-warning system for accounts whose predicted and actual billing diverge. The churn analysis isolated a clear high-risk segment, customers paying over $75/month with less than 20 months of tenure, concentrated among month-to-month contract holders, versus a stable "loyalty zone" beyond 30 months of tenure. These findings led to two concrete recommendations: proactive loyalty offers for high-bill customers within their first year of service, and contract-upgrade incentives to shift month-to-month subscribers toward annual plans.

REC-06

IBM Attrition Analytics, AI-Powered Retention Strategy

Power BI DAX Star Schema AI Visuals

The Business Problem: Employee attrition is a costly, disruptive problem, it drives up recruitment and training costs, breaks team continuity, and often signals deeper issues around compensation, workload, or career growth. HR needed to know which employees were most likely to leave, and why, in time to act proactively rather than reactively.

The Solution: Built an interactive Power BI report following the CRISP-DM methodology on the IBM HR Analytics Employee Attrition dataset (1,470 employees, 35 attributes). Cleaned the data and built a star-schema model, a central Employee fact table joined to Rating Scale Lookup, Age Band, and Department dimensions, then authored DAX measures including Attrition Rate, Average Monthly Income, and a custom Flight Risk indicator. Layered in Power BI's AI-powered visuals, Key Influencers to automatically rank the strongest drivers of attrition, and a Decomposition Tree for interactive top-down drill-through, across three report pages with full cross-filtering and conditional formatting.

Strategic Impact: The analysis found Sales Representatives at a 39.8% attrition rate, more than double the next-highest role, and employees who work overtime roughly 3× more likely to leave. Leavers earned 30% less on average than stayers within the same job level ($4,787 vs. $6,833 monthly), and low work-life balance nearly doubled attrition risk (31.2% vs. 14.2%). Career stagnation proved non-linear: employees in their first two years in a role were 3.5× more likely to leave than 11+ year veterans. The report flagged 39 employees as high flight risk against an estimated $5.4M in annual attrition-related costs, translating into five targeted recommendations, from a Sales overtime audit to a structured onboarding track and a pay-equity review.
