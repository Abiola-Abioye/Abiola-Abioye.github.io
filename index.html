REC — 01

Retail Inventory Automation & Markdown Optimization

Data Modeling Python (pandas/numpy) Power BI DAX Framework

The Business Problem: Frontline retail teams routinely execute manual shelf-rotation checklists to catch expiring inventory. This process is time-intensive, prone to human oversight, and often collides with peak front-end checkout traffic, leading to lost productivity and increased waste (shrinkage).

The Solution: Deployed a priority-driven, exception-based inventory tracking model. Engineered a custom 3-month retail transactional dataset using Python (pandas, numpy) and architected a highly scalable, normalized Star Schema database inside Power BI by abstracting a unique batch lookup dimension to break complex routing loops.

Relational Star Schema
Automated Operations UI

Strategic Impact: Completely replaces blind manual scanning with a predictive, targeted markdown picklist. By implementing automated time-intelligence thresholds, the dashboard surfaces high-risk stock and applies a tier-decay markdown strategy (25%, 50%, 75%) to maximize product salvage recovery while freeing up labor hours for high-traffic customer waves.

REC — 02

Employee Timesheet Data Warehouse — SCD Type 1 & 2

SQL Server Data Warehousing Star Schema ETL

The Business Problem: Employee records change over time — contact details update, employees change departments or get promoted — but historical timesheet data still needs to reflect the correct department and role for the day the work was actually done, not just an employee's current profile.

The Solution: Built a staging-to-warehouse ETL pipeline in SQL Server around a star schema (DimEmployee, FactTimesheet). Implemented Slowly Changing Dimensions logic: Type 1 overwrites for low-impact fields like email and phone, and Type 2 versioning for department and job title changes — expiring the old record and inserting a new one with its own effective date range. Timesheet facts are then mapped to whichever employee version was active on the date the work occurred.

Strategic Impact: The model preserves accurate point-in-time reporting instead of overwriting history. For example, the warehouse can correctly show that one employee's hours split 28.57% under her old department and 71.43% under her new one after a transfer — a distinction a simpler "current state only" table would erase entirely.

REC — 03

Financial Planning & Forecasting Model — NaturaGlow

Excel Goal Seek Scenario Analysis Forecasting

The Business Problem: NaturaGlow needed to know whether a new product line would be profitable before committing to it — but the answer depended heavily on volatile assumptions like unit price and expected sales volume, and a colleague's early sales estimate hadn't yet been checked against actual historical trends.

The Solution: Built a dynamic financial model in Excel with base-case profit and break-even calculations, then used Goal Seek to solve backward for the unit price and sales volume needed to hit specific profit targets. Layered in a two-variable sensitivity data table to stress-test profit against simultaneous swings in price and volume, and used Scenario Manager to compare Pessimistic, Most Likely, and Optimistic outcomes across two pricing tiers. Cross-checked all of it against a 5-year historical sales trendline forecast.

Strategic Impact: The base case revealed a projected $5,000 loss, with break-even actually requiring 1,250 units sold monthly. Goal Seek showed a $70 price point (up from a $50 base) would hit a $15,000 profit target, while at the base price, 2,250 units would be needed for a $20,000 profit. Scenario analysis found only the Most Likely High ($27,120) and Optimistic High ($31,880) scenarios cleared a $25,000 profit bar. The trendline forecast also showed a colleague's initial 1,000-unit sales estimate was overly conservative against the model's 1,303-unit "Most Likely" projection — meaning the business was positioned to outperform their planning baseline by over 30%.
